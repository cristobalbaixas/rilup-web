Limits

What we guarantee, and what we do not

Rilup verifies the origin of a capture, not the honesty of the scene in front of the lens. This page lists what the seal proves, what it cannot prove, the attacks we know about and what we do about each. It is written to be argued with: if you find a hole, write to us.

What the seal proves

  • The file was produced by the official Rilup app, unmodified, running on a device that Google (Play Integrity) or Apple (App Attest) vouched for at the moment of capture.
  • A hardware-backed key on that device signed the file's SHA-256 fingerprint, together with a server-issued nonce, at capture time. On Android the key lives in the Keystore (StrongBox or TEE); on iPhone, in the Secure Enclave.
  • The published bytes are exactly the ones that were signed. The server recomputes the fingerprint on upload and refuses anything that does not match.
  • The capture happened inside a short window measured by our server's clock and Google's or Apple's, never by the phone's.
  • Edits are declared layers (cuts, text, color look) applied at playback. The original is never modified and anyone can view it.

What it does not prove

  • What was in front of the lens. A staged scene, a costume, a trick of perspective or a rehearsed take are all real captures. The seal says "this camera saw this"; it does not say "this is what it looks like".
  • Who is in the picture, or who pressed the button. Rilup verifies content, not identity.
  • Anything about a file that left Rilup and was altered. Once a copy is recompressed or edited elsewhere, only the perceptual match at rilup.com/verify can say "this looks like a verified capture", with a distance, never "this is it".

Attacks we know about

AttackCan it be done?What we do
Pointing the camera at a screen that shows a fakeYes, with effort This is the hard one and we say so. We measure physical signals at capture time (a torch pulse and how the scene responds, focus distance, and depth maps on iPhone) plus image analysis of moiré, flicker and bezels. It is a detector, not a verdict: suspicious captures go to human review, and confirmed cases cost the account. We are calibrating it with real-versus-screen pairs and we expect it to keep improving; we do not claim it is complete.
Uploading a file from the gallery or another appNo There is no code path for it. The in-app camera is the only input, and the server only accepts files whose fingerprint was signed at capture.
Modified app, emulator or replayed capturesNo, on supported devices Play Integrity and App Attest verify the app's identity and the device's integrity; each capture is bound to a single-use nonce and a time window, so an old capture cannot be registered twice or late.
A rooted phone that makes the secure chip sign as if it were the genuine appYes, on some devices, with root This is public and we will not pretend otherwise: a phone rooted through a vulnerability can be made to pass attestation and sign a file with the real hardware key, with the app untouched. Attestation alone cannot see that, and nobody honest claims it can. So we do not rely on attestation alone. Every capture records the device verdict and the certificate shows its level. The physical signals taken at capture (torch response, focus distance, motion sensors, depth on iPhone) have to be consistent with a scene in front of a lens, not with a file. Captures are looked at per device, so a device that produces what no other device produces stands out. A device key can be revoked and everything it signed quarantined, and a confirmed case costs the account and the device, after human review. We do not promise that no phone can be broken. We promise that breaking one buys a single phone, that what it produces can give it away, and that it gets revoked.
Phones without Google services or with custom ROMs (for example GrapheneOS)Not supported today Play Integrity fails there, so captures from those devices are rejected rather than accepted without proof. We know this excludes privacy-focused users; supporting hardware attestation without Google services is on the list, not done.
Compromise of Rilup's serversWould be serious The signing keys never leave the devices; the server verifies, it does not sign captures. Certificates are public per post, so a tampered server could be caught by comparing what it says against the signed data. We store the attestation evidence and the fingerprints; a breach could expose metadata, not forge captures.
Sharing a real capture with a false caption or in a misleading contextYes Nothing technical prevents it. The certificate shows when and, if the author chose, roughly where the capture happened, so the context can be checked. Caption and context are the reader's job, as with any medium.
Copies recompressed by WhatsApp, Instagram or emailVerification survives The exact fingerprint breaks, so rilup.com/verify also matches a perceptual fingerprint and reports the distance. A copy that is heavily cropped, rotated or altered will not match, and we prefer that to a false positive.

What happens when something breaks

A detector or a report never punishes on its own. Every case goes to human review, the person is told what happened and why, and can appeal. A confirmed authenticity violation resets the Reality Level to zero and, on repeat, suspends the account and bans the device. Captures signed by a revoked key can be quarantined in bulk.

This page describes the system as deployed in the current beta. We update it when the system changes, and we would rather list a weakness here than have it found elsewhere first.
