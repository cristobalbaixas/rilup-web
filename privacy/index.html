Rilup Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2, 2026 · English version published October 29, 2026 · Controller: Rilup SpA (Chile) · Contact: admin@rilup.com

Rilup is a social network where the only content that can be posted is captured live with the in-app camera and cryptographically verified at the moment of capture. This policy explains what data we process, what for, and what control you have over it.

1. Data we collect

  • Account: email (to sign in), username, display name, bio and profile photo (which is always a selfie captured in the app).
  • Content: the photos and videos you capture and post, their captions, hashtags and mentions, and the declared edits (crop, exposure, trims, layers).
  • Capture verification: for every capture we record a cryptographic fingerprint (SHA-256) of the file, an integrity verdict about the app and the device issued by Google Play Integrity on Android or by Apple App Attest on iPhone, a reading of the motion sensors (gyroscope and accelerometer) and the camera settings for that shot (zoom, lens, lighting, stabilization). This data exists to prove that the content is real and, above all, to avoid accusing you by mistake: without knowing whether you used zoom or stabilization, an analysis could confuse a legitimate capture with a filmed screen.
  • Device identifier: on Android we store an identifier that the system assigns to Rilup on your phone (SSAID). It belongs to this app only, it cannot be used to track you in other apps, and it exists for a single purpose: so that a permanent suspension for fraud cannot be bypassed by creating another account on the same device. It resets if you factory-reset the phone. On iPhone the same role is played by the identifier of the hardware key that Apple attested for Rilup on your device.
  • Social activity: who you follow, likes, comments, saves, reports and direct messages.
  • Preferences: the interests and disciplines you declare for discovery.
  • Location: only if you turn it on, and it is off by default. The camera has a location switch: while it is off we never read your position. If you turn it on, your position is taken at capture and goes into the same signed text as the time and the file's fingerprint, so it cannot be added or corrected afterwards.

    What we keep, and what we do not. Your precise position is not stored. Before storing it we round it to a cell of about 110 meters, and the precise value is discarded. We also store whether the system flagged that position as mocked.

    And what gets published is coarser still. If you choose to show where it was, that coordinate is rounded again to a cell of about 500 meters, and that is the only one that goes out to other people. The difference exists because the risk is not in one post but in the set: many posts from your home, all at 110-meter precision, would say where you live. At 500 meters they do not. On the certificate that zone is drawn as a circle, not a point, to make clear it is not an address.

    We round to a fixed grid, not with random offsets. That is deliberate: with random offsets, averaging several posts from the same place could recover the exact point, while with a grid every capture in the same cell always yields the same value, and posting more often reveals nothing more.

    Two different uses, and the distinction matters. As proof, it serves to answer a single closed question: whether a capture happened inside a geofenced event. That use never shows your coordinate to anyone. As content, it is what is shown on the post, and that is the only one other users can see.

    It is decided twice and both times by you. Once at capture, by turning the switch on, and again at publish, where "Show where it was" is off by default. Only if you turn it on do other people see the place name, the rounded coordinate and a map of that zone. You can turn it off any time from the post, and doing so deletes the map, the name and the published coordinate.

    Private zones. You can define zones where the app never attaches location. The check is done by your phone before signing, so the coordinate of a private zone never leaves the device.

    Without permission, without a GPS fix, or inside a private zone, you capture and post all the same: the only thing you do not get is the verified seal at an event.

We do not collect your address book, we do not track your activity outside Rilup, and we do not use third-party advertising identifiers.

2. Original material of captures

When you post, the complete original file of the capture is uploaded (not the edited version), because that is the basis of verification. Other users see the version you published and can view the published segments without edits ("View original"). The material you trimmed out is not visible to other users: it is accessible only to our moderation team when a post is reported and must be reviewed. You can choose, post by post, to make the complete original visible.

2-bis. Automatic analysis of posts

When you post, we extract a few frames from your capture and analyze them automatically for two purposes: to detect whether another screen was filmed, and to detect material forbidden by the content rules. The content analysis uses Google Cloud Vision (SafeSearch), to which those frames are sent for processing; Google acts as a processor and does not use them for its own purposes.

No automatic result sanctions on its own: at most it queues the post for a person to review, and in high-confidence cases it may hide it temporarily while that review happens. You can see the results that led us to review your content by requesting them in writing.

2-ter. Verifying files outside the app

Anyone can drop a photo or video at rilup.com/verify to check whether it is a Rilup capture. The file is read in your browser: only its fingerprints (the exact SHA-256 and a perceptual fingerprint) are sent to our server, never the file itself. We do not keep a record of who verified what.

3. What we use the data for

  • Operating the service: posting, showing the feed of the people you follow, messaging.
  • Verifying the authenticity of content and preventing fraud (the core of the product).
  • Moderating: reviewing reported posts and applying the authenticity policy.
  • Discovery: showing you content from the interests you declare. Rilup does not build hidden behavioral profiles for the feed of the people you follow.

4. Who we share with

With the infrastructure providers needed to operate: Supabase (database, storage and authentication), Google (Play Integrity API, for the integrity verdict on Android; distribution through Google Play) and Apple (App Attest, for the integrity verdict on iPhone; distribution through the App Store and TestFlight). We do not sell personal data. If in the future we offer information to brands, it will be aggregated and anonymized, never individual.

With other users, when you choose it: the approximate location of a post on which you turned on "show where it was". It is the only location data that goes out to other people, and only if you turn it on.

To draw the map on that certificate we use MapTiler, with data from OpenStreetMap. The image is requested by our server, once per post, and stored: your phone never talks to the map provider, so neither MapTiler nor anyone else can know who looks at which place. And the only thing the provider receives is the coordinate already rounded to the 500-meter cell.

5. Retention

Your data is kept for as long as you have an account. Unpublished drafts live only on your phone. Verification records are kept in anonymized form (with no link to your identity) as evidence of the system's integrity.

6. Your rights

You can access, correct and delete your data. The app includes "Delete my account" (Profile → Settings), which permanently deletes your profile, your posts, your messages and your files. You can also write to admin@rilup.com to exercise any right (Chilean Law 21.719 and equivalent regulations, including those of your country of residence).

7. Minors

Rilup is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age required in your country). Our child safety standards explain what is forbidden, how to report from the app and which authorities we notify.

8. Security

Data travels encrypted (HTTPS) and access is restricted by policies at the database level. Integrity validations are always performed on our servers.

9. Changes

If we update this policy, we will publish the new version here with its date.
